A restorative circle gives a group a shared structure for taking turns, listening, and reflecting. Begin with a purpose that participants can understand and agree to. A circle for getting to know a group calls for different preparation than a conversation about a specific harm.

Before you meet

  • Name the purpose and explain roles, time, and what participation involves.
  • Ask about access needs in advance. Offer time to read the prompt, take a break, or contribute in a different way.
  • Talk about what participants may share afterward. Be specific about any limits to confidentiality.

During the circle

Try an opening prompt about what would make the conversation useful. Invite each person to speak or pass without explaining their choice. Give participants time to think. If someone needs to pause or the conversation moves beyond the agreed purpose, stop and decide together what should happen next.

Close and follow up

End with one chance to name what participants are taking away. When the group makes an agreement, clarify who will do what, when it will happen, and how participants will revisit it. Keep only records that the group has agreed are appropriate.
